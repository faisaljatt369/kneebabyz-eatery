Draft development plan

Kneebabyz Eatery Website and Owner App

Prepared October 13, 2026  |  Draft 1  |  Client meeting: October 14, 2026  |  Prepared by Faisal Jatt for Growing With Systems

1. Overview

Kneebabyz Eatery is a pop-up eatery and caterer based in West Oakland, California, with about 10,200 Instagram followers. The goal is a professional website and a simple owner app that lets customers see where the pop-up is today, order food, and request catering, while giving the owner an easy way to update the daily location and review requests.

The look follows the client's brief: red, black and yellow, with the Instagram logo and photos.

2. Scope

Public website

  • Home page with today's location banner
  • What We Do, Where to Find Us, How It Works
  • Food menu with photos and online ordering
  • Photo gallery and Instagram links
  • Catering quote request
  • FAQ, contact details, hours

Owner app

  • Secure owner login
  • Set today's location by address or GPS
  • View incoming orders
  • View catering requests
  • Installable on a phone home screen

Out of scope for the first version

  • Delivery tracking, loyalty programs and gift cards
  • Native iOS or Android store apps (the owner app is a web app)
  • Custom reporting beyond a simple orders list

3. Phased plan

Dates are estimates and depend on how quickly the client provides content and approvals.

Phase 1: Client meeting October 14

  • Present the live demo and walk through each feature.
  • Collect the content and decisions listed in sections 5 and 7.
  • Ask the client to approve linking their Square account.

Phase 2: Real foundation About Oct 15 to 17

  • Move hosting from GitHub Pages to a host that can run server code, under the dedicated company account.
  • Set up the database (Supabase) with row-level security on every table.
  • Build the owner login and save the location on the server so every visitor sees it.
  • Connect the catering form to a server that validates each request, emails the chef, and sends the lead into GHL.
  • Make the owner page installable on a phone.

Phase 3: Ordering and payments About Oct 17 to 21

  • Connect Square in sandbox mode and test with fake payments.
  • Calculate prices on the server so a customer cannot change what they pay.
  • Show orders in the owner view and send the owner an alert.
  • Switch to the client's real Square account once approved.
  • Add the client's DoorDash storefront link.

Phase 4: Content and launch About Oct 21 to 24

  • Add the real menu, prices, photos, logo and hours.
  • Connect a custom domain and basic search settings.
  • Test on phone and desktop, including the full order and catering flows.
  • Hand over a short guide for the owner: setting location, viewing orders.

4. Technology

PartChoice and reason
WebsiteNext.js with TypeScript. One codebase for the site and the owner app.
HostingVercel or Cloudflare under the company account (decision needed). The demo currently runs on GitHub Pages.
Database and loginSupabase, with row-level security and policies for select, insert, update and delete.
PaymentsSquare, using the client's existing account.
Catering emailA transactional email service (such as Resend) to the chef's inbox.
Leads and follow-upGHL receives each catering request as a lead through a webhook.
Delivery ordersDoorDash, linked from the site.

Security

  • No secrets in the code. Square, email and database keys live only in environment variables.
  • Every form and order is validated again on the server, even though the browser also checks it.
  • Only the owner can change the location or view orders. This is enforced by the database, not by hiding buttons.
  • The catering form includes a hidden field to catch bots.

5. Content needed from the client

  • Official business name
  • Full menu with real prices, and photos of each dish
  • Original logo file and the best original photos
  • Confirmed regular spots, days and hours (currently taken from flyers)
  • Phone number and email for customers (currently taken from Instagram)
  • DoorDash storefront link
  • Other social media handles, if any (Instagram and Threads are linked now)
  • Permission to use the Instagram photos on the website

7. Decisions needed

  1. Hosting account: Vercel or Cloudflare, under which company account.
  2. Order handling: pickup at the pop-up, DoorDash delivery, or both.
  3. Catering deposits: take a deposit at booking, or quote first and invoice later.
  4. First-version scope: confirm which features are in the first release and which come later.
  5. Owner alerts: email, text message, or both for new orders and requests.
  6. Domain name: which name to register.

8. Risks and how we handle them

RiskMitigation
Client is slow to send content or approvalsLaunch with clearly marked placeholders, replace them as content arrives.
Square access is delayedBuild and test everything in sandbox first, so going live is a short switch.
Photo quality and usage rightsRequest original files and written permission before launch.
Location or spots are out of dateOwner updates the live location daily. Regular spots are reviewed with the client.
Scope grows during the buildAgree the first-version scope in writing (decision 4) and log extras for a later release.
Fake orders or spamServer-side validation, the bot trap field on forms, and rate limiting.

9. Acceptance checklist

  • A customer can place a test order and pay through Square sandbox.
  • Order totals are correct and cannot be changed from the browser.
  • The owner can log in, set today's location, and the banner updates for every visitor.
  • A catering request reaches the chef's email and appears as a lead in GHL.
  • The site works on phones and desktop, and the owner app installs on a phone.
  • No secrets in the code, and row-level security is on every table.
  • The owner has a short guide and knows how to update the location.
